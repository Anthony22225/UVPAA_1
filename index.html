01 START WITH THE WATER

What is in
wastewater?

Every drop of wastewater contains a mixture of substances. Some are pollutants that can harm ecosystems or human health, while others are dissolved organic matter (DOM), a mixture of complex, soluble organic molecules and colloids. To protect our oceans, we need to eliminate pollutants in wastewater before discharge.

02 A REACTIVE LITTLE REAGENT

Meet peracetic acid.

Peracetic acid (PAA) is a powerful oxidant used in water treatment. An Advanced Oxidation Process (AOP) is a treatment method that produces highly reactive species capable of breaking down contaminants.

An atom is the basic building block of all matter in the universe. Atoms are linked together by chemical bonds (ionic, covalent) to form a compound. PAA is a covalent compound with carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms linked together with covalent bonds.

PAA contains a peroxyacid group (−COOOH). Compared with ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH), PAA contains one additional oxygen atom and an oxygen-oxygen peroxide bond. This weak peroxide bond requires less energy to break and makes PAA much more reactive than ethanoic acid.

CHAPTER SUMMARY

PAA differs from ethanoic acid because it contains one additional oxygen atom and a peroxide bond. This peroxide bond gives PAA stronger oxidative properties and makes it useful in wastewater treatment.

03 MAKE THE OXIDANT ACTIVE

Switch on
the light.

UV/PAA treatment is an Advanced Oxidation Process. UV light supplies energy that activates PAA molecules. Activated PAA molecules break apart and generate radicals.

Radicals are highly reactive atoms or molecules that contain an unpaired electron. Because radicals are unstable, they quickly react with nearby substances, including pollutants in wastewater.

04 NOT EVERY RADICAL FINDS A POLLUTANT

A competition
for radicals.

Radicals and oxidants are limited resources. Both pollutants and dissolved organic matter can consume them. Therefore, competition occurs between DOM and pollutants, influencing treatment efficiency.

05 CHANGE IS NOT THE SAME AS DISAPPEARANCE

DOM changes
shape.

Dissolved Organic Matter (DOM) molecules do not disapeear after reacting with radicals. Instead, they transform into other substances through oxygen addition and decarboxylation.

06 ONE PROCESS, MANY CHANGES

Put the story
together.

Wastewater contain pollutants and dissolved organic matter (DOM). Peracetic Acid (PAA) is a strong oxidant that is used in wastewater treatment. In UV/PAA, PAA fragments into radicals, which could remove pollutants, under UV light. However, DOM could also react with radicals through oxygen addition and decarboxylation to form undesired products that might worsen the water quality. It means that DOM would compete with pollutants for the radicals, reducing the efficiency of pollutant removal.
